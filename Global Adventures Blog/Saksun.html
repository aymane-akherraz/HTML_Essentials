Saksun, Denmark (Faroe Islands)

Hidden deep inside a natural amphitheater on Streymoy Island, Saksun feels like an outpost at the very edge of the world. My visit to this remote valley was a lesson in slow travel—a place where the dramatic moods of North Atlantic weather dictate your day, and the vibrant green landscape heals the soul.

The Iconic Turf-Roofed Valley

Saksun is famous for its centuries-old settlement, Dúvugarðar, which functions as a living museum. The structures are famous for their grass-covered roofs, designed to blend seamlessly into the mountain slopes while providing natural insulation against the fierce Atlantic rainstorm.

A Story of Solitude and Sea Storms

While exploring the valley, the notorious Faroese fog rolled in within seconds, completely blanketing the peaks and reducing visibility to just a few feet. I took shelter near the historic stone church overlooking the tidal lagoon.

A Simple Culinary Discovery

Cold, damp, and thoroughly windswept, I retreated to a small local setup nearby where I was introduced to hot Skwera (Faroese rhubarb tea) paired with freshly baked bread and salted butter. It wasn't a gourmet meal, but sitting in a stone cottage listening to the rain roar outside made it one of the most comforting culinary discoveries of my life.
